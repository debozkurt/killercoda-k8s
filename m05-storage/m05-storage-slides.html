Storage
that outlives the Pod

How a Pod gets durable storage — PersistentVolumeClaims, PersistentVolumes, StorageClasses, dynamic provisioning, and access modes — and the three places on that path where a Pod stops before it ever runs.

The failure lands on the Pod — Pending, never starting — but the cause is one or two objects away. kubectl get pvc reads the claim, and the claim says why.

What you'll walk away with

  • The claim/volume split — a PVC is a request for storage, a PV is the real volume, a StorageClass is the template that provisions one to satisfy the other
  • The storage chain — Pod → PVC → StorageClass → PV → attach → mount, and what owns each hop
  • The get pvc triage — one command splits a storage-stuck Pod three ways: the claim is absent, Pending, or Bound but unattachable
  • Provisioning — dynamic vs static, and why WaitForFirstConsumer holds a healthy claim Pending
  • Access modes and reclaim policy — RWO / ROX / RWX / RWOP and the Multi-Attach failure; why delete pvc can destroy data

A request, a volume,
and the template between them

A container's own filesystem is ephemeral — restart reverts it to the image, delete the Pod and its writes are gone. Durable storage is a separate object the Pod only references by name.

PersistentVolumeClaim

A request for storage: a size, an access mode, optionally a class. The workload owns it; the Pod names it by claimName.

scopenamespaced

PersistentVolume

The real volume — a cloud disk, an NFS export, a local directory. It exists independently of any Pod.

scopecluster-wide

StorageClass

A named recipe: which provisioner to call, with what parameters, at what reclaim policy and binding mode.

roleprovisions a PV on demand

The Pod references only the claim, in its own namespace — it never names a PV. That indirection is the design: the Pod stays disposable while the claim is the durable handle to its data.

One storage path, three places it stops

Each hop is owned by a different object. Read where the Pod stopped and you know which one to inspect.

Read the claim first: the Pod's status only says it's stuck; the claim's STATUS says why.

Where the volume comes from

Dynamic provisioning

The PVC names a StorageClass; creating the claim triggers that class's provisioner to create a matching PV on the spot — sized and configured to fit. No admin in the loop.

whenthe default path

Static provisioning

An admin creates PV objects ahead of time; a claim binds to whatever pre-existing volume matches on size, access mode, and class.

whenpre-provisioned pools

Name a storageClassName that doesn't exist — a typo, an uninstalled class — and there's no provisioner to call. No PV is created; the claim sits Pending forever and its Pod never starts. describe pvc names it: storageclass ... not found.

storageClassName is immutable once the PVC exists. The fix is delete and recreate — safe while the claim never bound, a data-migration problem once it did.

kubectl get pvc is the first look

The claim's STATUS column splits every storage-stuck Pod — before you reach for Pod logs that don't exist yet.

# always the first command on a storage-stuck Pod kubectl get pvc -n cdr-storage NAME STATUS VOLUME CAPACITY ACCESS MODES STORAGECLASS cdr-data Bound pvc-9f2a 1Gi RWO local-path # a Pod stuck Pending — the claim it names isn't in that list kubectl describe pod cdr-writer-0 -n cdr-storage persistentvolumeclaim "cdr-dat" not found

Three readings: Bound (storage is fine — look elsewhere), Pending (the claim can't get a volume), or the claim you expected isn't listed at all (a claimName typo, or a claim in another namespace — PVCs are namespaced).

Not every Pending claim is broken

The one exception that trips people — a claim held Pending on purpose, and healthy.

Immediate

Binds the PV as soon as the PVC is created, wherever the volume happens to land.

bindsat claim creation

WaitForFirstConsumer

Delays binding until a Pod actually uses the claim, so the volume is placed on the Pod's node. The default on most modern classes.

bindswhen a Pod consumes it

A WaitForFirstConsumer claim with no consuming Pod shows Pending and describe says waiting for first consumer — that is normal. It is broken only once a Pod is trying to use it and it still won't bind. One question separates them: is a Pod actually using the claim yet?

Access modes count nodes, not Pods

The word that trips people is "Once" — it means one node, not one Pod. Two Pods on the same node can share an RWO volume; two on different nodes cannot.

ModeShortWho may mount it
ReadWriteOnceRWOone node, read-write — Pods on that node share it
ReadOnlyManyROXmany nodes, read-only
ReadWriteManyRWXmany nodes, read-write at once
ReadWriteOncePodRWOPexactly one Pod, read-write

The access mode is a request the volume plugin enforces at attach time. RWX is what genuinely spans nodes — it needs network file storage behind it, not a block disk.

A Bound claim can still strand the Pod

After it binds, a volume is made usable in two steps — attach (make it available to a node), then mount (expose it in the container). Attach is where the access mode bites.

The setup

An RWO-backed Deployment scaled so two replicas schedule onto two different nodes. The first node attaches the volume — exclusively.

The second replica

asks for that same RWO volume from another node. It can't have it — the Pod hangs ContainerCreating while its claim still reads Bound.

block volumeMulti-Attach error
local volumevolume node affinity conflict

RWO is doing what it promised, not failing. Stop asking one volume to span nodes: run a single node-bound consumer, or move to RWX if replicas genuinely must share it across nodes.

reclaimPolicy decides what a delete destroys

When a PVC is deleted, the StorageClass's reclaim policy decides the PV's fate.

Delete

Destroys the underlying storage along with the PV. The data is gone. The default on most dynamic classes.

on delete pvcstorage destroyed

Retain

Keeps the PV and its data, moving it to Released for an admin to recover by hand.

on delete pvcdata preserved

kubectl delete pvc is not harmless cleanup — on a Delete-policy class it is a data-destruction command, and the claim looks like a lightweight request object, which makes the blast radius easy to underestimate. Put anything you can't lose on Retain.

What to carry forward

  • A PVC is a request, a PV is the volume, a StorageClass provisions one from the other. The Pod names only the claim — that indirection keeps it disposable while its data persists.
  • kubectl get pvc is the first look, and it splits every storage-stuck Pod three ways: the claim is absent, Pending, or Bound but the Pod's still stuck.
  • Not all Pending is broken. WaitForFirstConsumer holds a claim Pending until a Pod uses it — broken only if a Pod is trying to use it and it won't bind.
  • Access modes count nodes, not Pods. RWO is one node at a time — a shared RWO volume can't back replicas on two nodes. RWX is what spans nodes.
  • reclaimPolicy: Delete makes kubectl delete pvc a data-destruction command. Put anything you can't lose on Retain.
